EXPEDITION 007 · JUL 2026 · AMERICAS

Yellow in
Every Corner

"The beauty of any sport: even two strangers strike a conversation with ease."

Where Football Found Me

My connection with football started during childhood in Calcutta. Summer was football's season; winter belonged to cricket for a month or two, and then back again. International football in those years meant one thing: the World Cup, arriving every four years, enormous and anticipated. We watched it live on television, mostly during the middle of the night, sleep abandoned without much debate. There was no question of missing it.

So when the World Cup was announced for the United States, I was genuinely excited — and decided immediately I would go for a match or two. What I hadn't yet reckoned with was the reality of ticket prices. I had been thinking about the final, right next door at MetLife. That particular plan required a rethink.

✦ THE BRACKET ✦

Group C, Group I, Match 76

It started with a bracket obsession. Weeks before a ball was kicked at MetLife, I was deep in group standings, tracing possible paths: Group C, Group I, Match 76, Match 78 — working out who might actually appear in New Jersey by the knockout rounds. The group stage was fun to follow, but the smart decision was to wait. Higher stakes, better teams, same stadium. I had already passed on a group-stage ticket for Brazil versus Morocco and watched that one from the couch, tracking the standings while the groups played themselves out.

One path kept looking more promising than the rest: if Brazil topped Group C and the results in Group F and Group I broke the right way, Brazil could land back at MetLife for the Round of 16 on July 5th. I ran the numbers, watched the standings shift week to week, and when the moment felt right — before the match was officially confirmed — I found a ticket in the fair range. Then I waited.

The Ticket Before the Confirmation

I watched the groups close out, watched Match 76 and Match 78 take shape, and it landed exactly where the math said it might: Brazil versus Norway. Round of 16. MetLife Stadium. July 5th. The weeks of bracket tracking had held; the early ticket had been the right call. Two of the most exciting teams left in the tournament, and the fixture to match: Vini versus Haaland.

✦ MATCH DAY · JULY 5 ✦

Yellow at the Grocery Store

From the morning, people were showing their allegiances. On a grocery run before the match, I bumped into a woman walking her dog — she was wearing a Brazil jersey. I told her we were winning today. She said "of course," with a smile, and kept walking. That is one of football's particular gifts: it turns two strangers into allies in the space of three words.

Every Shade of Yellow

I had lunch at home, then walked to the subway for a train to Penn Station. Entering the station felt like stepping into a sea of yellow. My carriage was almost entirely Brazil supporters — a few of them speaking in a language I couldn't quite place, possibly Portuguese — the conversations already running at full energy. NJ Transit did a good job; the journey was smooth, the platform transitions unhurried. The colours only intensified as we pulled in. Yellow in every corner, with a few deliberate dots of red.

I usually aim to arrive around gate opening on any match day, and this one was no exception. There was time to soak in the build-up: the noise finding its shape in the concourses, the flags being sorted, the particular kind of anticipation that hangs in the air before a knockout match and doesn't feel like anything else.

✦ FIELD NOTES ✦

What the Bracket Taught Me

Football has a way of carrying you across years. A childhood spent watching midnight World Cups in Calcutta leads, eventually, to a train carriage full of yellow rolling toward New Jersey — and the distance between those two moments feels like it was always the point.

  • Buy the ticket before the match is confirmed — if you've done the bracket math and it holds, trust it.
  • Skip the group stage for the knockouts when the stadium is the same; the difference in atmosphere is significant.
  • Football turns strangers into a conversation in seconds. Expect to talk to everyone wearing the same colours.
  • Arrive at gate open. The build-up is part of the match.
  • NJ Transit from Penn Station on World Cup match day: smooth, cheerful, and entirely Brazil-coloured.
