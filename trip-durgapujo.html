"In Calcutta, the countdown to the next Pujo begins the very day this one ends."

The Mother of All Festivals

For a Bengali who grew up in Calcutta, Durga Pujo is not one festival among many; it is the mother of them all. We wait an entire year for it, and the waiting starts the day it concludes. This October I was home for it again.

The festival runs for ten days, and in other parts of India the same season is celebrated as Navaratri. In Hindu mythology, Durga Utsav marks the triumph of good over evil: the goddess Durga's victory over the shape-shifting buffalo demon. It is also a celebration of feminine divine power, and in Calcutta that idea is not abstract; it is built, painted, lit and carried through the streets by an entire city. In December 2021, Durga Puja in Kolkata was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity, recognising the sheer scale of its community craftsmanship, art and music.

✦ HOME ✦

Parents, Brothers, and Everyone Else

What makes Pujo exciting for me, every single year, is who it brings together. This is the time I spend with my parents, my brothers and my closest friends, and the time I reconnect with members of the extended family I don't see often enough the rest of the year.

📷 Photo coming · Family

Although the festival spans ten days, we celebrate mostly from the fifth day through the tenth. Each of those days follows a happy rhythm: we indulge in good food, and then we go pandal hopping.

The Menu My Parents Plan

The good food begins at home, and it begins with a plan. My parents map out what they will cook for me over the next three days, every meal from breakfast to dinner, and they do it with so much love and affection. I have never seen planning like it anywhere else. Not eating what they have made would be nothing less than a crime.

✦ PANDAL HOPPING ✦

Cities Built of Bamboo

A pandal is a temporary structure, a human-made replica framed in bamboo and hand-crafted with decorations that have been planned for months. Each one houses the goddess for the days of the festival, and each neighbourhood's pandal is an act of collective ambition. The creativity and hard work invested in a single pandal is unimaginable unless you witness it with your own eyes and listen to the stories of the people who made it.

📷 Photo coming · Pandal

I do most of my pandal hopping during the day. The evenings are overwhelmingly crowded; picture the crowd around the Rockefeller Center Christmas tree, then multiply it by five. Daylight lets you actually stand in front of the work and take it in.

The Lights

And then there are the lights. The illuminations that go up across the city during Pujo are, quite simply, beyond mind-boggling. If the pandals are the city's sculpture, the lights are its night-time painting.

✦ OLD FRIENDS ✦

From Boyhood to Fatherhood

The pandals are only one part of Pujo. Catching up with childhood friends is the other, and for me it is just as exciting. It is something to see how the boys I grew up with have become fathers, and to watch them look after their families, their parents and their friends, all within such a packed festival schedule. That they make it look effortless is something I genuinely admire.

✦ FIELD NOTES ✦

What Pujo Holds

Durga Pujo is a celebration of a goddess's victory, of a city's craftsmanship, and of a year's worth of waiting. But for me it is, above all, a homecoming: the one stretch of the year when family, old friends and the city I grew up in are all in the same place at the same time.

If you ever go, go pandal hopping by daylight, and ask someone to tell you the story behind the pandal you're standing in front of.

"The goddess goes home on the tenth day. The rest of us start counting toward her return."
